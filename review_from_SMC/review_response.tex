\begin{revresponse}
	We agree that the previous version did not sufficiently clarify why weight constraints and input constraints should be considered simultaneously, nor did it clearly distinguish the proposed method from BLF-based methods, projection algorithms, and our prior work.

	In the revised manuscript, we have substantially revised the Introduction and the contribution statement.
	We now explicitly explain that weight constraints and input constraints serve different purposes in NAC: weight constraints regulate the learning dynamics of the neural approximator and prevent excessive growth of adaptive weights, whereas input constraints represent actuator feasibility and safety-related admissible control regions. 
	Existing methods usually address these constraints separately. Projection algorithms mainly restrict the adaptive weights but do not directly encode actuator constraints, whereas auxiliary-system-based and saturation-function-based approaches mainly handle input saturation but are typically tailored to element-wise box constraints. BLF-based methods are more commonly used for state, output, or tracking-error constraints and do not directly provide a unified mechanism for multiple convex input constraints in the DNN adaptation law.

To clarify the novelty, we added a new discussion in Section I comparing CONAC with projection algorithms, BLF-based methods, auxiliary-system-based NAC, and optimization-based methods. We also revised the contribution statement to emphasize that the proposed CONAC formulates the DNN weight adaptation as a constrained optimization problem in which both weight constraints and multiple convex input constraints are incorporated as inequality constraints and handled through primal-dual adaptation laws.

We further clarified the distinction from our previous studies. Compared with our prior work, the present manuscript extends the framework from shallow neural networks and norm-type constraints to DNNs under multiple convex input constraints, generalizes the admissible input set to the intersection of continuously differentiable convex constraint functions, and validates the proposed method through real-time implementation on a two-link robotic manipulator.
